The Seven Sacraments

What is a sacrament?

Sacraments are sacred, visible rites and rituals instituted by Jesus Christ that act as outward signs of an inward, divine grace.

Explore the Sacraments

Sacraments of Initiation

The Sacraments of Initiation are foundational rites that lay the groundwork for the Christian life, welcoming individuals into the Church, deepening their bond with Christ, and fully incorporating them into the faith community.

01 Baptism

This sacrament cleanses a person from original sin and welcomes them into the Christian faith community. Water is poured or used for immersion as a symbol of spiritual rebirth and purification. It is considered the gateway to all other sacraments because it marks the start of a believer's spiritual journey. Galatians 3:27 — "For all of you who were baptized into Christ have clothed yourselves with Christ."

02 Confirmation

This sacrament deepens the grace received at Baptism and seals the believer with the gifts of the Holy Spirit. Through the laying on of hands and anointing with sacred chrism oil, the individual is strengthened in their faith. It marks a mature commitment to living out and defending the Christian life. 2 Corinthians 1:21–22 — "Now it is God who makes both us and you stand firm in Christ. He anointed us, set his seal of ownership on us, and put his Spirit in our hearts..."

03 Eucharist

In this sacrament, believers receive the consecrated bread and wine, which Catholics believe become the real body and blood of Jesus Christ. It re-enacts the Last Supper and serves as the central act of worship in the Mass. By consuming the Eucharist, Christians nourish their spiritual life and strengthen their unity with Christ and the Church. John 6:54 — "Whoever eats my flesh and drinks my blood has eternal life, and I will raise them up at the last day."

Sacraments of Healing

The Sacraments of Healing continue the earthly healing ministry of Jesus Christ to mend human brokenness, forgive sins, and offer spiritual or physical strength.

04 Reconciliation

This sacrament offers forgiveness for sins committed after Baptism through a sincere confession to a priest. The believer expresses true sorrow, receives absolution, and performs an act of penance to repair spiritual harm. It restores a broken relationship with God and heals the individual's soul. John 20:22–23 — "And with that he breathed on them and said, 'Receive the Holy Spirit and forgive sins.'"

05 Anointing of the Sick

Administered to those facing serious illness, old age, or major surgery, this sacrament provides spiritual strength and peace. A priest prays over the person and anoints their forehead and hands with blessed oil. It offers comfort, forgiveness of sins, and sometimes physical restoration if it aligns with God's will. James 5:14–15 — "Is anyone among you sick? Let them call the elders of the church to pray over them and anoint them with oil in the name of the Lord."

Sacraments of Vocation

The Sacrament of Vocation directs individuals toward the salvation of others, conferring a specific mission to build up and serve the Church community.

06 Matrimony

This sacrament establishes a sacred, lifelong covenant between a man and a woman in the presence of God and the community. It grants the couple the divine grace needed to support each other in unconditional love and fidelity. The union is intended for the mutual good of the spouses and the loving upbringing of children. Matthew 19:6 — "So they are no longer two, but one flesh. Therefore what God has joined together, let no one separate."

07 Holy Orders

This sacrament ordains men as bishops, priests, or deacons to serve the Church and lead its spiritual mission. Through the laying on of hands by a bishop, the ordained individual receives special grace to preach, administer sacraments, and shepherd the faithful. It ensures the continuation of Christ's ministry through apostolic succession. 1 Timothy 4:14 — "Do not neglect your gift, which was given you through prophecy when the body of elders laid their hands on you."
